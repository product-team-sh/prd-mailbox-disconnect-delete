1

Problem

Clicking Disconnect or Delete on a mailbox shows one generic sentence – "your all sequences using this email account will be paused" – no count, no names, no severity. The user can't tell whether that means one paused follow-up or two hundred stranded prospects, so they either abandon the action or proceed blind. Nobody could state the damage: a $2,500 escalation ran 104 comments with every disconnection reported by the customer, never proactively (measured, parent §3).

The problem the fix creates. Typed-confirmation friction on every Disconnect/Delete, including harmless ones, trains users to type past a warning without reading it. No gate at all when nothing depends on the mailbox (§5.1) is the answer.

2

Why this approach

Parent PRD §7.2 already chose the shape: per-sequence impact and consent at the moment of removal, over a single aggregate warning, blocking removal outright, or notifying after the fact. This doc specs the first cut of that – visibility and a consent gate – not the full surface.

Why now, not the full surface at once. Email Account Disconnected fires 52,794 times / 4,148 users; Email Account Deleted fires 30,032 times / 1,554 users (measured, parent §4) – the destructive path is heavily used and carries zero visibility today. Per-sequence remediation (reassign to another mailbox, pause, continue reduced) needs a mailbox-picker and per-sequence capacity recalculation nobody has built yet. Visibility alone answers the loudest complaint without waiting on that.

Will not claim: that this reduces churn by a stated amount (parent §5 already disclaims a causal churn read from sharing depth). Sample sequence names, counts and capacity figures in the design are illustrative, not real account data.

3

What we're building

One screen, one gate, three trigger states.

  1. Before Disconnect or Delete proceeds, the user sees every sequence the mailbox serves – split by whether sending stops completely (no other sender) or merely slows (other senders remain), stopping sequences listed first.
  2. Proceeding requires typing the mailbox's email address, whenever there's any impact. A mailbox with zero active sequences confirms with one click instead.
  3. Invariant: the product never removes a mailbox without having shown its impact. Where impact can't be computed, the action is blocked – never silently allowed.

Deliberately narrow. Per-sequence remediation (reassign/pause/reduce) is a later slice – this screen shows the cost, it doesn't yet offer a remedy. The Connected toggle keeps its current one-click behavior; only Disconnect and Delete route through this screen.

Surfaces

5.1Mailbox removal – impact & consent

What it's for. The user is on Email Accounts, opens a row's kebab menu, clicks Disconnect or Delete. This screen stands between that click and the action happening.

What it must do

  • Fetch impact live at the moment of the click. Never cached (parent A2.7, release blocker).
  • Zero active sequences → plain confirm, no typed gate.
  • Otherwise, list every sequence the mailbox serves, as a scrolling list – accounts run up to 3,142 sequences on one mailbox (measured, parent §4), so this can't be a page that grows with account size.
  • Sole-sender sequences ("stops completely") sort first, red; sequences with another healthy sender remaining ("slows down") amber.
  • Per sequence: pending prospects, pending follow-ups, and either the reason ("Only sender") or capacity today → after.
  • Pending in-thread follow-ups called out as unrecoverable. Only specced for sole-sender rows today – likely wrong. Same-thread binding is to whichever mailbox originated that prospect's thread, not to the sequence's sender roster (parent §2.3) – a "slows down" sequence could still strand specific prospects if this mailbox started their threads. Needs the fetch to report in-thread counts per sequence regardless of bucket, and the warning to show wherever that count is nonzero. Flagged, not fixed – resolve before estimation.
  • Proceed stays disabled until the mailbox's email is typed, case/whitespace-insensitive, whenever there's impact.
  • A short badge next to the gate: "Reversible – reconnect anytime" (Disconnect) or "Permanent – reconnecting means setting this mailbox up from scratch" (Delete).
  • Fetch failure → block entirely, offer Retry, never fall back to an unguarded confirm.
  • On confirm: state what happened – how many sequences stopped vs. slowed.

Outcomes it must guarantee

  • No removal without impact shown and the typed match confirmed, whenever impact > 0. (Read-back: removal_confirmed always follows a removal_impact_shown for the same mailboxId – §7.)
  • Displayed counts match the database at the instant of display.
  • A blocked fetch never degrades into an unguarded confirm.

Replaces parent A2.3, A2.4 and A2.11-F, which assumed per-sequence remediation choices and the toggle path – neither exists in this slice.

Cross-cutting

  • No removal proceeds without the gate. Release blocker (R-3, R-9).
  • No displayed impact figure is cached. Release blocker (inherited, parent A2.7).
  • Capacity figures must use the outreach-only field, never the aggregate chip. Release blocker – blocked on which field is sequence-usable capacity (below). Resolve before estimation.
  • A blocked fetch never silently allows removal. Release blocker.

Still open, still blocking (inherited from the parent PRD, unresolved there too):

  • Which field is sequence-usable capacity – every capacity figure on this screen is wrong until this is named. Rajat to confirm.
  • Does account-level Disconnect/Delete already auto-pause dependent sequences, or does this need a new transition? Assume not; verify on a 2-mailbox test account.
  • How exactly is the daily allowance split across sequences? The "capacity today → after" figure is uncomputable without it.

None of these block the rest of the screen shipping – they block the Capacity column and the estimation of R-1/R-2 specifically.

7eSuccess criteria – day 30, 100% exposure

  1. Every removal carries a recorded impact-shown event first. Non-negotiable.
  2. Abandon rate is measurably non-zero. Iterate if not met, don't revert.
  3. Zero removals completing without the gate. Non-negotiable – this design has no unguarded path.
